## If you only have 30 seconds

> Every name comes out of one score. It rewards games that are proven and that subscribers
> don't already own — two things that pull against each other. One weight was forced by the
> data: Fit sits at ten percent because it predicts worse than the average. One was a
> judgement call. So we tested the judgement — move it as far as seventy-thirty and the top
> ten doesn't change.

## Do not say

- **Anything about engagement, playtime or retention.** There is no playtime data in the
  source — every column is zero. The claim was withdrawn, not softened.
- "Scarcity contributed three percent" — the measured influence figure is 0.030 on a
  correlation scale, not a percentage. Say "almost nothing."

## If asked

**"Why 50/40 and not 60/30?"** — No reason, and that's why we tested it. Both give the same
top ten. What actually matters is Fit's weight, and that one was derived.

**"Is Headroom really a separate signal?"** — Partly. SteamSpy reports owners in buckets,
not numbers, so inside any one bucket Headroom collapses onto Recognition exactly. Its real
job is moving titles between three ownership bands, not ranking within one. It's on the
slide because we'd rather state it than be caught by it.

**"So it's just a most-reviewed list?"** — Close to it, banded by ownership, and we say so.
The filters upstream are where the work is: five thousand reviews, seventy percent positive,
under 750,000 owners, small developer. Those decide who gets scored at all.
