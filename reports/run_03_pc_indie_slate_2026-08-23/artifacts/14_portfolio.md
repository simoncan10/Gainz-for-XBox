# Stage 14 — The Game Pass portfolio: 24 named titles, four tiers

**The recommendation, in one sentence:** license these twenty-four named back-catalogue
titles into Game Pass, starting with the six Microsoft has already licensed once, and take
them in tier order because that order is ranked by how cheaply and how certainly each can
be executed — not by score.

This recommends **no studio acquisition, no studio funding, no pricing change and no new
development**, all of which the goal statement places out of scope. Licensing existing
back-catalogue titles is the only version of this that fits Xbox's stated post-July-2026
direction (`04_context.md` §1).

Structured data, one row per title with full provenance:
`artifacts/14_portfolio.json`. Generated by `scripts/14_build_portfolio.py`, which
**joins** `12_candidates_v2.csv` to `13_availability.json` on `app_id` and aborts if a
title is missing from either — so no row in the portfolio was typed by hand.

---

## What objective this portfolio optimises

Xbox's stated direction is **margin discipline and retention**, not subscriber reach. The
basis is `04_context.md` §2 [ANALYSIS, confidence MEDIUM]: the July 2026 closure of Ninja
Theory, Double Fine and Compulsion, plus the April 2026 Ultimate rollback from $29.99 to
$22.99 after churn. Both point the same way.

Two consequences, and they are the reason this portfolio looks the way it does:

1. **Breadth of small, high-recognition, low-ownership titles beats one large swing.**
   Retention is served by many reasons not to cancel. The single sourced internal Xbox
   assessment of a large day-one swing — Star Wars Jedi: Survivor at ≈$300M — was flagged
   internally as poor ROI [SOURCED: Axios, "Microsoft leak reveals cost estimates of
   bringing big releases to Game Pass," 2023-09-19, secondary reporting of a
   legal-discovery leak].
2. **The AAA-scale option is named but ranked last within its tier.** Persona 3 Reload is
   pick 6 of 6 in Tier 1; Marvel's Guardians of the Galaxy is held as the Tier 1 alternate.
   Twenty-two indie-scale titles rank ahead of them. That is a deliberate ordering, not an
   oversight.

### The rotated-out question, handled openly

Eight of the top thirty were previously on Game Pass and left. That is the single most
interesting finding in the evidence, and there is a real argument each way.

**Why it could be the strongest part of the portfolio:** every commercial and technical
obstacle has already been cleared once. The rights holder has said yes. The Xbox SKU
exists and passed certification. A contract template exists. And Microsoft holds
first-party performance data on the exact title's exact prior run — which no other tier
enjoys.

**Why it could be the weakest:** titles leave Game Pass for reasons, and both candidate
reasons are bad news. Either **the publisher declined renewal**, in which case the price is
above what Microsoft was willing to pay and re-approaching means paying more; or
**Microsoft declined renewal**, in which case Microsoft's own engagement data already
returned a verdict on this title and the verdict was no.

**We do not know which, and no external source establishes it for any of the eight.** The
honest position is that this is an unresolved unknown, not a resolved advantage.

**Why the tier still leads the portfolio despite that.** The asymmetry is that Microsoft
can settle this unknown at **zero external cost and within one day**, from a dataset it
already owns and we do not. Every other tier's central unknown requires a negotiation to
resolve. So Tier 1 goes first in the room not because the evidence is strongest, but
because it is the tier the board can validate or kill immediately using what is already on
its own servers. **That is a condition attached to a named recommendation, not a
substitute for one** — the six titles are the recommendation, and they stand as picks if
the board chooses not to run the check.

**Tier removal rule.** Remove any title here the moment Microsoft's internal record of its
prior run puts it in the bottom quartile of engagement-per-licensing-dollar among
comparable back-catalogue titles.

**Named alternate: Marvel's Guardians of the Galaxy** (app_id 1088850, v2 rank 28, 35,789
reviews, 500k–1M owners). Rotated out March 15 2023 after a 12-month run. It is the
alternate rather than a pick because **the counterparty changed**: Embracer Group bought
Eidos-Montréal and the IP from Square Enix in 2022, so the prior yes was given by a company
that no longer holds the rights — and a licensed Marvel property carries a second rights
holder this analysis has not screened.

## Sizing — what we can and cannot say

**We cannot give a per-title price, and we will not invent one.**

**[SOURCED]** The only public figure whose range actually includes this tier: Iain
MacIntyre, former Xbox business development lead, states the Game Pass deals he negotiated
ranged from **$50K to over $50M across 500+ deals**, covering both indie and AAA. There is
**no public breakdown of what drives the low end versus the high end**. [TweakTown,
2025-07-13, relaying a LinkedIn post — a primary quote via secondary press.]

**[SOURCED]** Every other public number is the wrong tier: day-one AAA at $5M (Baldur's
Gate 3) to $300M (Jedi: Survivor), and GTA V back-catalogue at $12–15M/month. [Axios,
2023-09-19.] Extrapolating downward from these to a sub-750k-owner indie title would be
speculation, and Stage 13 flagged it as such.

**What we do instead.** The portfolio is **ordered so the cheapest-to-verify and
already-proven cases come first**, which makes the recommendation robust to not knowing the
price. Tier 1 requires no port, no certification and no cold introduction. Tier 2 requires
no port. Tier 3 requires one status check. Tier 4 requires a port that may not exist. The
board can commit tier by tier and stop wherever the quotes stop making sense.

**A trap this stage explicitly refused.** Retail price is **not** used as a cost proxy or a
cost ordering anywhere above. Stage 11 (RT-04) established that retail price in this
catalogue is a monotone proxy for *production budget and press coverage* — Metacritic
presence rises 5.7% → 22.5% and median reviews 152 → 864 across price bands — so ranking on
inverse price ranks on absence of budget. Price appears in the JSON as
`price_usd_retail_not_licence_cost` and does no work in this portfolio's ordering. The cost
ordering is by **deal structure** (prior deal exists / port exists / status known /
counterparty scale), not by sticker price.

## What is weak about this portfolio, stated before the board states it

- **[MEASURED] It is concentrated in singleplayer narrative.** Across the 24 picks:
  Indie 19/24 (79.2%), Adventure 15/24 (62.5%), but Action only 4/24 (16.7%) and
  multiplayer only 4/24 (16.7%). The 215-title qualifying list is Action 50.7% and
  multiplayer 30.7% [MEASURED, n=215]. **The top-30 slice is materially more
  singleplayer-narrative than the list it came from.** This matters because Xbox console's
  top genre in 2024 was sports and PC's was shooters [ESTIMATE: Newzoo via Plarium] —
  neither is represented here at all.
  **The named remedy, from the same list:** Streets of Rogue (512900, rank 43),
  Children of Morta (330020, rank 47), Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor (2321470, rank 40),
  SnowRunner (1465360, rank 54), TMNT: Shredder's Revenge (1361510, rank 58) — all carry
  co-op and multiplayer flags. **None has an availability verdict**, because all sit outside
  the Stage 13 top-30 screen. Extending that screen to rank 60 is the cheapest way to fix
  the concentration, and it would be fixed with named titles, not with a framework.
- **[MEASURED] 20 of 24 sit in the 500k–1M owner bucket** — the top of the qualifying
  range, right against the ≤750,000 ceiling. These are the *most*-owned titles that still
  qualify as "not already widely owned." That follows mechanically from Recognition carrying
  45% of the composite while recognition and ownership correlate; it is not a bug, but the
  board should hear it before it asks.
- **[MEASURED] Developer concentration is not a problem:** the maximum is 2 titles from one
  developer (Shiying Studio: Firework, Sanfu), n=24.
- **Fit is honestly weak.** Out-of-sample Pearson r **0.377–0.388** across five seeds, which
  is why it carries only 20% and why nothing above leans on it. The two directly measured
  pillars carry 80%.
- **No engagement or playtime data exists in this dataset** — every playtime column is zero.
  Nothing above claims anything about retention or session length for a specific title.
- **Owners are bucketed SteamSpy estimates**, not measured sales. Reviews are self-selected.
- **[DERIVED] The data is Steam PC; the recommendation is Xbox console.** Console ARPPU is
  **+47.3%** ($81.68 ÷ $55.47 − 1 = 0.4725) [inputs ESTIMATE: MIDiA Research 2024 via
  Plarium]. The `has_controller_support` gate addresses control scheme only; it does not
  address certification history or store discovery.
- **12 of 24 picks (50.0%) carry a Metacritic score** [MEASURED, n=24], against 48.0% of the
  638-title eligible pool. Metacritic is never an input to the composite, so this is
  independent corroboration rather than a restatement — but it is corroboration for only
  half the list.

## The one rule for removing any title from this portfolio

A title leaves the portfolio when **any single verified fact makes the licence unbuyable,
unnecessary, or unplayable on Xbox console**: the rights are held by a party who will not
grant a console subscription licence; the title is already in the subscription; Microsoft
already owns it; or no native Xbox console SKU exists and none is dated. Everything else —
a weaker score, an unconfirmed status, a higher-than-hoped quote — moves a title **down a
tier**, not off the list. Per-title triggers are in `14_portfolio.json`
(`removal_trigger` on every row).
